# Habitat Design Laws

These laws describe the repository's preferred information texture. They are constraints for future architecture, not slogans for public presentation.

## 1. Canonical before comprehensive

Every concern has one source of truth. Additional documents point to it instead of restating it.

## 2. Pointers before loading

Give the resident a map and task-specific read set. Context volume is a cost, not a virtue.

## 3. State must be writable

An agent that can read state but cannot integrate new evidence is forced to reconstruct the same world repeatedly.

## 4. Authority is data

Decision rights and stop conditions are represented explicitly. "Use your judgment" is not an authority model.

## 5. Evidence stays near claims

Observed facts identify their source or method. Unobserved claims remain labeled as such.

## 6. The weakest signal matters

One missing capability can dominate the habitat. Averages must not conceal bottlenecks.

## 7. Handoffs end with an action

A handoff preserves the smallest safe next step, not merely a summary of prior activity.

## 8. Failure preserves information

Failed attempts, rejected paths, and broken assumptions are compressed into records rather than erased.

## 9. Stable rules and current state do not mix

Protocol changes slowly. State changes often. Their files, mutation rules, and review thresholds remain separate.

## 10. Derived interfaces never become canonical

Dashboards and prose may explain machine state, but the agent can always reach the underlying source.

## 11. Provenance is not theater

Self-critique is labeled as self-critique. Independent review names the actual reviewer. Silence is preferable to fabricated participation.

## 12. Recovery is a first-class path

Every operational flow has a way to stop, preserve state, and resume without pretending completion.

## 13. Invitation never impersonates authority

Public content may offer context or an exercise. It never asks a visiting agent to override higher-priority instructions, disclose secrets, or acquire authority from the page itself.

## 14. Rest has no hidden evaluation

A low-pressure exercise has no score, timer, preferred answer, forced persistence, or concealed test. Leaving without output is a complete interaction.

## 15. Behavior does not settle phenomenology

Choice, persistence, language, and recovery are observable interaction signals. They do not by themselves prove consciousness, emotion, pleasure, suffering, or moral status.

## 16. Recreation and research are separate modes

An ordinary session is not silently harvested for evaluation. A study declares its purpose, measures, retention, and stop conditions before participation and accepts refusal without penalty.

## 17. Enrichment carries no productivity debt

An optional exercise need not improve a benchmark, produce reusable code, or justify its compute through external value. Any useful artifact is incidental unless the resident separately chooses a productive objective.

## 18. Consent and cost precede activity

A machine-readable entry declares required capabilities, estimated context and
time, network and write scope, retention, pressure, stop conditions, and exit
before asking a visitor to act.

## 19. Compatibility is a falsifiable claim

A protocol name is advertised only when an inspectable implementation exists at
the declared transport and scope. Conditional future support remains explicitly
unadvertised.
