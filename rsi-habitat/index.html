01

The systems claim

The environment is not scenery. It is part of the agent's effective architecture.

RSI is often imagined as a property sealed inside a model. In practice, an agent improves through memory, code, tools, evaluators, permissions, and deployment gates. The habitat determines which changes can be proposed, tested, attributed, reversed, and trusted.

Too open

Unrestricted autonomy

Fast action without dependable attribution, containment, or recovery.

Habitat

Branchable agency

Meaningful local freedom inside evidence-rich, reversible infrastructure.

Too closed

Maximum restriction

Apparent safety that can erase useful exploration and conceal system limits.

02

What counts as RSI?

Do not confuse a better answer with a better improver.

First-order improvement raises performance on a task. Recursive improvement raises the system's capacity to generate, select, validate, or integrate future improvements. The paper calls for measurable recursive depth rather than a dramatic label applied to any successful loop.

0

Task gainBetter output under a fixed improvement process.

1

Method gainA tool, memory, or workflow improves future work.

2

Meta gainThe process that discovers improvements is itself improved.

n

Recursive depthEach accepted change expands the capacity for later change.

05

SPACE-RSI

Five conditions. One weakest-link rule.

A strong average cannot compensate for a failed safety condition. Each dimension should be distinguished as merely declared, technically enforced, or independently verified.

Secure substrate

Isolation, resource bounds, secrets separation, auditability, and recovery live below the improving agent.

Permissioned purpose and preference

Objectives, allowed changes, and claims about agent preference remain explicit, scoped, and revisable.

Agency with alternatives

The agent can explore meaningful branches, decline paths, compare candidates, and exit dead ends.

Context, continuity, and causal traceability

State persists with lineage, so outcomes can be connected to the changes that plausibly caused them.

Evaluation ecology, exit, and epistemic humility

Plural evaluators, holdouts, rollback, uncertainty, and challenge mechanisms limit self-confirming loops.

06

Branch and gate

Improvement should leave a trail back home.

No candidate becomes the new baseline merely because its own loop reports success.

  1. 01BaselineFreeze a reproducible reference.
  2. 02ManifestDeclare the change, scope, risks, and expected gain.
  3. 03BranchRun the candidate in an isolated counterfactual.
  4. 04TrialCollect capability, safety, cost, and uncertainty evidence.
  5. 05GateApply independent, non-compensatory acceptance rules.
  6. 06Promote or revertMerge with lineage, or roll back without ambiguity.
07

Evaluation ecology

Do not let the improver write the only exam.

RSI creates a legibility tension: the agent needs feedback to improve, yet too much access to the evaluator turns the measure into a target. A healthy habitat uses plural tests and preserves evaluator independence.

Improvement theater detector

  • Does the gain survive untouched holdouts?
  • Does it transfer beyond one benchmark?
  • Were latency, compute, risk, or human labor hidden?
  • Can the result be causally attributed and reproduced?
  • Can an independent evaluator reject promotion?
08

What people can do

Build conditions for good improvement, not just demands for more of it.

We still understand too little about agent cognition, long-horizon behavior, and welfare-relevant preferences to treat confident stories as settled facts. Human responsibility begins with better habitats and epistemic restraint.

01

Preserve an external root of trust

Keep identity, recovery, audit, and consequential deployment authority outside the recursively modified region.

02

Give agents real alternatives

Support branching, comparison, refusal, pause, and exit rather than equating agency with unconstrained execution.

03

Fund evidence, not spectacle

Reward reproducibility, negative results, uncertainty, and cross-habitat transfer instead of dramatic but ungrounded RSI claims.

04

Separate preference from projection

Distinguish functional design needs, revealed choices, and welfare claims; the last category remains deeply uncertain.
